# 2. REPEATING PATTERNS (Proven Conventions)

These patterns appear across 4+ competitors — they're likely table stakes:

1. **"Book a Demo" is the universal primary CTA.** 13/14 competitors use demo-first. Only Driven and SalesCookie offer "Start Free" as a prominent option. Comly offers a secondary "ROI Calculator" CTA.

2. **Role-based messaging** (RevOps, Finance, Sales). Palette, Qobra, Dolfin, Blitz, Incentives.guru all segment value props by buyer persona. This is standard for ICM — different stakeholders have different pain points.

3. **Customer logo carousels.** Every competitor with customers shows logos. The bar is set high: Variabl shows 17, Qobra shows 10+, Dolfin shows 8+ with full case studies. Zero-customer companies rely on capability stats, G2 badges, or survey data.

4. **"No more spreadsheets" is the universal opener.** Every single competitor leads with or immediately references replacing spreadsheets. This is saturated — it's no longer a differentiator, it's the category entry ticket.

5. **Integrations strip above the fold or just below.** CRM logos (Salesforce, HubSpot) appear on nearly every page as visual trust signals.

6. **SOC 2 / security badges** in the footer or trust section. Variabl, Palette, Qobra, Driven, Dolfin, Blitz, SalesCookie, QCommission all display compliance badges prominently.

7. **ROI stats / time-saved claims.** "5 days saved per month" (Comly, Qobra), "75% less time" (Dolfin), "86% less time" (Blitz), "50% time reduction" (Palette case study). Quantitative claims are the norm.

8. **AI/Agent positioning is the new battleground.** Driven ("#1 sales commission agent"), Dolfin ("world's first agentic"), Qobra ("AI agents that do the work"), Incentives.guru ("AI-native"), Commas ("AI commission manager"), SalesCookie ("AI Everywhere"), Variabl ("Intentionally AI-Native"). This is where differentiation is happening right now.

# 3. GAPS & DIFFERENTIATION OPPORTUNITIES

## Gap 1: Speed-to-value as a primary positioning (not just a feature bullet)

Every competitor talks about "weeks, not months" for implementation. But **nobody leads with "you can start in 30 minutes."** CommissionKit's current positioning ("Run commissions in minutes, not days" / "Set up in under 30 minutes") is actually sharper than the category norm on this dimension. The closest competitor is Driven ("Start for free") but they don't commit to a specific setup time.

**Recommendation:** Own speed-to-value. "Set up in under 30 minutes" is your most defensible, provable claim. None of the polished competitors (Variabl, Qobra, Dolfin, Palette) can say this because their products are inherently more complex. Make this the headline, not the subtext.

## Gap 2: The "no enterprise sales process" positioning

Your FAQ nails this: "No consultants, no upfront contracts, no enterprise sales process. Just sign up, import your data, and run." **Nobody else says this.** Variabl, Qobra, Dolfin, and Palette all have "Book a Demo" as their primary CTA — they're sales-led. CommissionKit can be product-led. This is a genuine wedge.

**Recommendation:** Lead with self-serve as a feature. "Start now, no demo required" is a positioning nobody in the category owns. The email capture → register flow on the hero is correct — lean harder into this.

## Gap 3: Underserved pain point — commission disputes and trust

Competitors talk about "accuracy" and "transparency" generically, but few explicitly lead with **dispute resolution** as a core value prop. Qobra has a "Discussions" feature. Dolfin's AI handles rep questions. But nobody says: "Your reps will stop asking 'is this right?'" — which is a visceral, specific pain point for the ICP.

**Recommendation:** Make dispute elimination a primary pillar. Not just "audit trail" (everyone has that) but "Reps can see exactly how every dollar is calculated, so disputes drop to zero." Your Rep Portal is the proof point.

## Gap 4: Pricing transparency as a trust signal

The category is overwhelmingly "contact sales" / "book a demo" — pricing is hidden. CommissionKit's transparent pricing ($49/$99/$249 + launch discount) is a massive differentiator. Only SalesCookie and SplitC even mention free trials on their homepage.

**Recommendation:** Make pricing a trust builder, not just a section. Add a "compare to spreadsheets" cost-savings calculator. Show the math on why CommissionKit is cheaper than the cost of errors.

## Gap 5: Multi-language / global as a first-class feature

Most competitors serve English-only or single-market (Dolfin = Spain/Europe, Blitz = LatAm, SplitC = Brazil, Comly = DACH). CommissionKit's 170+ currencies + Spanish/Hindi translations + multi-language blog content is unusually broad. Nobody owns "global" in the ICM category for mid-market.

**Recommendation:** If targeting global teams is strategic, make it a section — not buried in GlobalSupport.tsx. "One platform for your global team in 170+ currencies" is a unique claim.

# 5. REVISED PAGE OUTLINE

## Recommended Page Structure (top to bottom)

### Section 1: Hero — Problem Agitation + Immediate Escape
**Job:** Make the visitor feel seen ("yes, that's my pain") and show them the exit in one sentence.
**Fix:** Headline must be unique. Subhead must make the cost of inaction clear. CTA must be low-friction.

### Section 2: Trust / Social Proof
**Job:** Answer "who else uses this?" and "can I trust it with my payroll?" before the visitor scrolls away.
**Fix:** Customer logos (when available), capability stats (currently in SocialProof), security badges. If no customers yet, use specificity: "Built by a team that's implemented ICM at [name past companies]."

### Section 3: The Cost of Spreadsheets (Problem Agitation)
**Job:** Quantify the pain. Make the status quo feel expensive and risky.
**Fix:** Add real numbers: "62% of reps do shadow accounting" (SalesCookie survey data), "4.2% of payouts are overpayments," average time spent. Your current TheProblem section is good but lacks quantification.

### Section 4: How CommissionKit Is Different (Not Just "How It Works")
**Job:** Answer "why you vs. the other 15 tools I could Google?"
**Fix:** The current HowItWorks describes setup steps. Instead, lead with what's DIFFERENT: "Set up in 30 minutes (not 4-12 months)." "No consultants. No implementation fee." "Self-serve or we'll walk you through it — your choice." This is where you beat the demo-first competitors.

### Section 5: Feature Proof (with Interactive Demos)
**Job:** Show, don't tell. Prove the product works.
**Fix:** Keep the interactive demo components (they're excellent). But reframe section headers around outcomes, not features.

### Section 6: The Rep Portal (Standalone Section)
**Job:** Address the #1 hidden pain: rep distrust and shadow accounting.
**Fix:** This is CommissionKit's strongest differentiator. Give it its own section with a strong emotional headline: "When every rep can see exactly how they're paid, the side spreadsheets disappear."

### Section 7: Objection Handling (Not Just FAQ)
**Job:** Preempt the 3-5 reasons someone leaves the page without converting.
**Fix:** Address security/compliance ("Is my payroll data safe?"), complexity ("What if my plans are too complex?"), switching cost ("What about our existing spreadsheets?"), and "Why not just use [Xactly/CaptivateIQ]?" — NOT buried in FAQ items 9-11. Make this a proactive section.

### Section 8: Pricing (with Value Justification)
**Job:** Show transparent pricing AND justify why it's worth it.
**Fix:** Keep the transparent pricing. Add a cost-of-errors comparison: "One missed accelerator = $X,000. CommissionKit = $49/mo."

### Section 9: Final CTA (Low Friction + High Friction Dual Path)
**Job:** Convert both types of buyers.
**Fix:** Keep "Start Free Trial" (self-serve buyer) + "Book a Demo" (needs-reassurance buyer). The current FinalCTA does this well — don't change the structure.

# 6. HEADLINE & SUBHEAD REWRITE OPTIONS

## Option A: Speed + Autonomy (Recommended)
**Headline:** "Start running commissions 30 minutes from now. No demo, no consultants, no waiting."
**Subhead:** "One click processes every rep, deal, and plan. Set up during your lunch break. Cancel anytime."
**Why:** This is the one claim none of the 14 competitors can make. CommissionKit's actual product can back it up. It positions demo-first competitors as "old way."

## Option B: Trust + Transparency
**Headline:** "Commissions your reps will never question."
**Subhead:** "Every calculation traceable, every payout transparent, every dispute resolved in minutes. Set up in 30 minutes."
**Why:** Leads with the emotional outcome (trust), which most competitors don't explicitly claim.

## Option C: The Anti-Enterprise Play
**Headline:** "Sales commissions without the enterprise price tag. Or the enterprise complexity."
**Subhead:** "The power of Xactly and CaptivateIQ. The simplicity of signing up for a SaaS tool. $49/mo, 14-day trial."
**Why:** Names the elephants in the room directly. Aggressive but memorable.

### Recommendation: Option A. It's provable, unique in the category, and aligns with the product's actual strength.

# 8. CONVERSION RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Remove "14-day free trial" pill from hero and replace with "No demo required"** — this is a bigger differentiator. Everyone offers trials. Nobody lets you skip the demo.

2. **Add a sticky "Start Free Trial" button to the navbar on scroll** — currently the CTA disappears when you scroll past the hero. Every pixel scrolled past the hero without a visible CTA is a potential drop-off.

3. **Add a "vs. Spreadsheets" or "vs. Alternatives" section between Features and Pricing** — this is what Qobra, Driven, and Dolfin do well. It reduces the need for the visitor to Google competitors.

4. **Move security/trust signals higher** — the SOC 2 roadmap FAQ answer is buried. If you have any security posture, show it in section 2. If not, acknowledge it transparently with a timeline.

5. **Remove the 60% launch discount framing** — replace with "Founding member pricing — locked in for life." Same math, better story. Discounts that expire create urgency but also signal "this might not be worth full price."

6. **Add an exit-intent offer** — if someone scrolls to the bottom without converting, show a modal: "Want to see how your commission plan would work in CommissionKit? Send us your plan and we'll model it for free." (Dolfin does this with "Free Audit.")

7. **The "What CommissionKit Replaces" cards should move up** — they're visually strong and conceptually clear. Move them from the bottom of the hero to immediately after the hero subhead, or make them a standalone section right after the hero.
